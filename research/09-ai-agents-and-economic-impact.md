# AI Agents, Software Automation, and Economic Impact — State of Play, September 2026

*Research report. Evidence labels: **CONFIRMED** (primary source / official filing / peer-reviewed or published paper), **REPORTED** (credible journalism or secondary sourcing, not primary-verifiable here), **SPECULATION** (forecast, model, or opinion).*

---

## TL;DR

1. Coding agents crossed from autocomplete to autonomous multi-hour work. METR's revised Time Horizon 1.1 methodology puts the 50%-success task horizon of the best assessed models at roughly **16-20 hours**, with a post-2023 doubling time of **~131 days** — faster than the 7-month figure that anchored 2025 discourse [1][2].
2. SWE-bench Verified is effectively saturating: frontier models sit in the **80-81%** band, and the benchmark community has shifted attention to SWE-bench Pro, Terminal-Bench, and long-horizon suites [3].
3. The most-cited negative result in the field — METR's RCT showing experienced open-source developers were **19% slower** with AI — did not flip. METR's 2026 follow-up was **abandoned as uninterpretable** because too few developers would accept a no-AI control arm; the partial estimate held at ~18% slowdown with a wide interval [4][5].
4. AI writes a large and contested share of code: **~42%** of committed code by one survey, ~27% of production code by a large empirical study, and Google has claimed **~75%** of new code is AI-generated-and-human-approved [6][7].
5. Protocols consolidated. MCP is the de facto tool-connection standard (~**97M monthly SDK downloads**, donated to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation), A2A hit **1.0** in April 2026, and a five-layer agentic-commerce stack (AP2, UCP, ACP, Visa TAP, Web Bot Auth) formed on top [8][9].
6. Revenue is real and enormous. Nvidia's most recent quarter (Q2 FY2027, ended July 2026) was **$96.2B revenue, $89.0B data center, +106% YoY** [10]. Anthropic's run rate is **REPORTED** at ~$65B, OpenAI's at ~$40B [11][12].
7. AI capex is now a macro variable: ~**$1T** global AI investment in 2026, ~1.8% of US GDP, and AI-related investment accounted for roughly **74% of Q1 2026 US GDP growth** [13][14].
8. The bubble debate produced an actual event: a **June 2026 correction** erased ~$1.3T of semiconductor market value in a week, against Bain's **$800B by-2030 revenue-shortfall** framing and its 2026 finding that promised AI cost savings largely "didn't arrive" [15][16].
9. Labor evidence sharpened rather than exploded. Stanford's Canaries update (Aug 2026) finds **no economy-wide displacement**, but a **19% employment gap** for 22-25 year olds in AI-exposed occupations, widened from 15% a year earlier, driven by reduced hiring, not layoffs [17].
10. The honest synthesis: capability is compounding faster than measured firm-level productivity, and the gap between the two is where both the bull and bear cases live.
